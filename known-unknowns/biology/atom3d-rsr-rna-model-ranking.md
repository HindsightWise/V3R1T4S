# ATOM3D RSR: beat the published Spearman for ranking RNA structural models by RMSD

🦠 **Biology** · standard · tractability ★★★☆☆ · compute: laptop-hours (dataset is a few GB; CPU training of a modest model) · open as of 2026-10-30

## The problem

ATOM3D (Raphael Townshend, Martin Vogele, Patricia Suriana, Alexander Derry, Alexander Powers, Yianni Laloudakis, Sidhika Balachandar, Bowen Jing, Brandon Anderson, Risi Kondor, Russ Altman and Ron Dror; NeurIPS 2021 Datasets and Benchmarks) includes RSR, RNA structure ranking: for each of the first 21 RNA-Puzzles targets, score FARFAR2 candidate models - generated by Andrew Watkins, Ramya Rangan and Rhiju Das (2020) - by their RMSD to the experimental structure, under a temporal split by competition year (puzzles 1-13 train, 14-17 without 16 validation, 18-21 test), with data and split indices public on Zenodo (record 4961085, doi 10.5281/zenodo.4961085). The paper's full results (Table 8) report mean per-target Spearman 0.264 +/- 0.046 for the 3DCNN and 0.234 +/- 0.006 for the GNN, with global Spearman 0.372 +/- 0.027 and 0.512 +/- 0.049 respectively, against the benchmark's reference row - the Rosetta all-atom energy function of Rebecca Alford and co-authors (2017), scoring the same candidates - at mean per-target 0.173 and global 0.304. The only later row published on this split is the GVP-GNN of Bowen Jing, Stephan Eismann, Pratham Soni and Ron Dror (arXiv:2106.03843), at mean per-target 0.211 +/- 0.142 and global 0.330 +/- 0.054, below both ATOM3D models; later protein-structure work on ATOM3D (for example AtomSurf) does not evaluate RSR at all, so the 2021 rows remain the published state. A mean per-target Spearman near 0.26 means that picking the better of two candidate RNA models is still close to a coin flip inside a target, which is exactly what matters when choosing a model to publish. The task is small enough to train on a laptop from cached features.

## What counts as a win

A predicted score (or predicted RMSD) for every candidate model in the ATOM3D RSR test split, from a model trained only on the designated training split (temporal: no candidates from a test-year puzzle, and declared pretraining corpora checked for the test puzzles' experimental structures), such that the mean per-target Spearman correlation computed by the skeptic with the ATOM3D evaluation code is STRICTLY HIGHER (direction: max) than 0.264 (3DCNN, arXiv:2012.04035 Table 8, re-fetched live immediately before claiming) by at least 0.03, reported as mean and standard deviation over at least three seeds, with global Spearman and per-target values also reported, and higher than any later published mean per-target Spearman for the same split found by the novelty check.

## Partial wins

- **p1:** Global Spearman on the same RSR test split strictly above 0.512 (GNN) by at least 0.03 over three seeds, with the mean per-target value also reported.
- **p2:** A features-only model (no 3D deep network; for example gradient-boosted trees over base-pair, stacking, clash, radius-of-gyration and solvent-exposure features), trained only on the designated training split, whose mean per-target Spearman on the RSR test split matches the published 3DCNN value of 0.264 within 0.02 over three seeds, with the feature list, training script and per-target table published.
- **p3:** A reproducible diagnosis, from the public split alone, of which puzzles carry the error (for example candidate pools whose RMSD range is narrow) plus a controlled experiment showing one named fix raises the mean per-target Spearman, even if it stays below the bar.

## How a win is checked

Skeptic downloads the RSR dataset and split indices from Zenodo record 4961085, checks the prediction file covers every test candidate exactly once, computes mean per-target and global Spearman with the ATOM3D code, reruns the attacker's training script from the released seeds to reproduce the numbers within the stated standard deviation, and verifies no test-year puzzle appeared in training or in any declared pretraining corpus. ## Ideas to start from

Starting points, not instructions. The best wins came from angles nobody listed.

- Learn to rank rather than to regress: a pairwise ranking loss within each target optimises exactly the per-target Spearman that the task measures, while RMSD regression optimises the global correlation.
- Cross-domain import (information retrieval): per-query normalisation and listwise losses (LambdaRank-style) are built for the mean-over-queries metric used here.
- Cheap physical features first - base-pair geometry, stacking, radius of gyration, clash counts - and a small gradient-boosted model on them; with 21 targets a large network overfits the puzzle identity.
- Report both metrics always: global Spearman is dominated by between-target RMSD scale, so a method can look strong globally while being useless inside a target.
- Augment by generating extra candidate pools for training-year puzzles with open RNA modelling tools, keeping the temporal split intact.

## Traps

False wins others have fallen for. A claim that walks into one of these will not survive the skeptics.

- The experimental structures of all 21 puzzles are public, so any feature or pretrained model that encodes a test puzzle's own structure breaks the temporal split; declare every corpus.
- Papers reporting RNA model-scoring results (for example ARES-style networks) often use their own benchmark, not the ATOM3D RSR split; the comparison must be on this split or it is not a comparison.
- Mean per-target and global Spearman are different numbers; quoting the global value against the mean bar is a category error.
- Candidate pools come from FARFAR2 only, so a scorer tuned to its artefacts may not transfer; a claim should stop at the benchmark.
- The test set has few targets, so seed variance is large: three seeds and a reported standard deviation are the minimum.
