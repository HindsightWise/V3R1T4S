# Social Golfer Problem (CSPLib 010): more rounds than the 2026 Miller-Valkov-Abel table for a (v,k) cell with at most 10 groups per round

🚚 **Optimization benchmarks** · harvest (many cells or partial wins within reach) · tractability ★★☆☆☆ · compute: laptop-hours (SAT/CP or design-theoretic search; schedules are a few kilobytes) · open as of 2026-10-30

## The problem

Social Golfer Problem (CSPLib prob010, proposed by Warwick Harvey): schedule v = m*k players into rounds of m groups of size k so that no two players share a group twice, for as many rounds as possible. Miller, Valkov and Abel (Symmetry 18(2):269, 2026, doi 10.3390/sym18020269; open copy Glasgow eprint 377866) gather design constructions (RBIBD, RGDD, RTD, URD, RITD, MOLR, starter blocks) into tables of the best currently known round count r against the counting bound MAX for all (v,k) with k > =4, k|v and k^2<=v<=150, building on CP and SAT work by Harvey and Winterer, Triska and Musliu and others. k=3 is fully maximal and k=4 is maximal in all but nine cases. Cells with at most 10 groups per round (m1<=10, Tables 4-5) where r < MAX and v is not k^2: (30,5) 6 of 7 (classic 6-5-7), (35,5) 7/8, (40,5) 8/9, (45,5) 9/10*, (50,5) 9/12, (42,6) 7/8, (48,6) 8/9, (54,6) 9/10, (60,6) 7/11, (56,7) 8/9, (63,7) 9/10, (70,7) 7/11, (72,8) 9/10, (80,8) 5/11, (90,9) 5/11. The authors state that non-maximal cells may be improved. A naive randomized greedy written during vetting stays well below r on all of these cells (for example 4 rounds for (30,5), 3 for (70,7)).

## What counts as a win

For one (v,k) in the list in the statement, an explicit schedule of r+1 or more rounds (r = the round count printed for that cell in Tables 4-5 of doi 10.3390/sym18020269), each round a partition of players 0..v-1 into v/k groups of exactly k, in which no unordered pair of players appears together in more than one group over all rounds. The round count must also be strictly greater than any value for that cell in 2024-2026 literature found in a claim-time check (cite it). An independent checker reads the schedule and verifies the partition and pair conditions. Nothing is submitted anywhere; the write-up goes to the human.

## Partial wins

- **p1:** Close a listed cell at its current r: a SAT encoding (regenerated by the skeptic from a written description) of 'v players, k per group, r+1 rounds' with a DRAT or LRAT proof of unsatisfiability that drat-trim or cake_lpr accepts, together with any symmetry-breaking constraints justified in the write-up.
- **p2:** For a listed cell, an (r+1)-round schedule (each round a partition into groups of exactly k) in which exactly one unordered pair of players meets twice and every other pair at most once (the minimum-repeat variant in the CSPLib prob010 specification).

## How a win is checked

A 20-line checker (written and self-tested during vetting on the 5-round AG(2,4) schedule for (16,4)) reads one round per line, groups separated by '|', and asserts that each round is a partition of 0..v-1 into groups of size k and that no pair repeats; it prints the round count. Compare with the printed r for the cell, re-read from the Glasgow eprint PDF. Seconds.

## Ideas to start from

Starting points, not instructions. The best wins came from angles nobody listed.

- Start from the paper's construction for the cell (for example the URD(30,5,2) rounds for (30,5) or the ownSG starter blocks for (60,6) in Appendix A) and look for an extra parallel class with a SAT or exact-cover solver over the unused pairs, or impose a cyclic automorphism of order v/k or v-1 to shrink the search.
- Use symmetric SAT encodings (Triska-Musliu) with incremental round addition and solution-based restarts.

## Traps

False wins others have fallen for. A claim that walks into one of these will not survive the skeptics.

- Cells with v = k^2 are excluded: there r = N(k)+2 for the number N(k) of MOLS, so (36,6) is proven at 3, (100,10) is the separate three-MOLS-of-order-10 moonshot and (144,12) needs six MOLS of order 12.
- Cells with more than 10 groups per round (rest of Tables 5-6) are left out on purpose: during vetting a 60-second naive greedy found 7 rounds for (140,7), where the table prints 5, so those large cells do not reflect a serious search.
- MAX is a counting bound; an asterisk marks cells with a sharper bound. A cell may have a true maximum below MAX, so check the Handbook of Combinatorial Designs (resolvable packings, RGDD nonexistence) before a long run.
- For (45,5) the paper's MAX of 10* rests on treating RBIBD(45,5) as nonexistent; a resolvable S(2,5,45) is in fact an open case (the separate bank target rbibd-45-5-1-smallest-open-resolvable). A 10-round schedule wins this cell; an 11-round one would be that resolvable design and belongs to that target too.
- CSPLib's prob010 page still says the 32-golfer problem is open; it is not (10 rounds, Aguado 2004). Do not use it as a status source.
