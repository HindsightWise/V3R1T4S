# Chromatic numbers of lattices (Voronoi graphs): settle a 5-dimensional primitive lattice, the Coxeter-Todd lattice K12 or the Barnes-Wall lattice BW16

🕸️ **Graphs, games and automata** · standard · tractability ★★☆☆☆ · compute: laptop-days (SAT on finite induced subgraphs of Cayley graphs of Z^n; periodic colourings of discrete tori) · open as of 2026-10-30

## The problem

The chromatic number chi(L) of a lattice L in R^n is the chromatic number of its Voronoi graph, the Cayley graph on L generated by the strict Voronoi vectors (equivalently, colour the Voronoi tessellation so that cells sharing a facet differ). Dutour Sikiric, Madore, Moustrou and Vallentin (arXiv 1907.09751, J. London Math. Soc. 2021) settled all lattices of dimension 2 and 3, all irreducible root lattices and their duals (with chi(D_n) reduced to the halved cube) and the Leech lattice (4096), and named K12 and BW16 as the next natural cases, expecting the spectral bound to be close. Vallentin, Weissbach and Zimmermann (arXiv 2407.03513, Indag. Math. 2024) determined all 4-dimensional lattices (52 parallelohedra, 16 Voronoi-graph types, maximum 7) by finite-subgraph SAT lower bounds and periodic upper bounds, and report that for dimension 5 their computations 'remained inconclusive', the bounds being far apart; they also ask whether 2^(n-1) colours always suffice for n >= 3. Target: an exact, certified chromatic number for a new lattice.

## What counts as a win

Exact determination chi(L) = c, not published before, for L either (a) a 5-dimensional lattice whose Voronoi cell is one of the 222 primitive 5-dimensional parallelohedra (explicit Gram matrix) and whose Voronoi graph is not isomorphic to that of A_5^* (the permutohedron; chi(A_5^*) = 6 by arXiv 1907.09751), or (b) K12 or BW16 (explicit Gram matrix from the Nebe-Sloane catalogue). Upper bound: an explicit c-colouring of a discrete torus L/M (M a sublattice given by a basis) checked against every strict Voronoi vector. Lower bound: an explicit finite induced subgraph of the Voronoi graph certified non-(c-1)-colourable by an UNSAT result with a DRAT/LRAT proof checked by drat-trim or cake_lpr for a CNF the skeptic regenerates (as in arXiv 2407.03513), or, for (b) only, a written proof by a clique, the sphere-packing bound (Theorem 4.1 of arXiv 1907.09751) or a spectral bound, every numerical input certified in exact or interval arithmetic. The strict Voronoi vectors must be recomputed independently (Voronoi's criterion: v is strict iff +-v are the only shortest vectors in v + 2L). A literature search (citations of arXiv 2407.03513 and 1907.09751), rerun before claiming, must show the value not published before.

## Ideas to start from

Starting points, not instructions. The best wins came from angles nobody listed.

- Use sublattice cosets: if M is a sublattice with no strict Voronoi vector in M, then L/M gives a periodic colouring with [L:M] colours; search sublattices with SAT on L/kL for small k.
- Lower bounds: grow finite induced subgraphs greedily around a dense clique and use symmetric (automorphism-orbit) encodings; sphere-packing (Theorem 4.1 of 1907.09751) and spectral bounds give targets.

## Traps

False wins others have fallen for. A claim that walks into one of these will not survive the skeptics.

- The Voronoi graph uses strict Voronoi vectors (facet vectors), not all vectors of the two shortest norms.
- Isomorphic Voronoi graphs from different Gram matrices are the same problem: check combinatorial type before claiming a new lattice.
