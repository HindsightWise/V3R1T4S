# Cuneiform sign detection on the open MaiCuBeDa 3D-rendering benchmark: beat a reproduced RepPoints baseline (AP@50/AP@75) on a fixed, period-filtered split

🏺 **Ancient texts and fragments** · harvest (many cells or partial wins within reach) · tractability ★★☆☆☆ · compute: laptop-hours · open as of 2026-10-28

## The problem

The Mainz Cuneiform Benchmark Dataset (MaiCuBeDa Hilprecht, heiDATA doi:10.11588/DATA/QSNIQ2, CC BY-SA 4.0, version 1.1 released 2025-08-06 with description dated 2023-08-29, its 11 files (ids, sizes, MD5) unchanged from version 1.0 released 2023-08-30; about 9.3 GB: three ~3 GB zips (MSII / VirtualLight / Mixed) that hold only char/line/word/wedge crops and W3C JSON-LD annotation files, not whole-segment renderings, plus annotations.ttl, line/word/translit metadata; Journal of Open Archaeology Data 2025, doi:10.5334/joad.172) is the open benchmark for locating cuneiform signs on 3D-scan renderings. Stotzner, Homburg and Mara (ICCV 2023 workshops, arXiv 2308.11277) train a RepPoints/ResNet-18 single-class detector on 512x512 patches cut from 873 segments of 490 tablets (10,311 patches, 21,228 sign annotations) with a 2:1:1 segment-level split, and report in Table 2 (VOC 11-point interpolated AP, section 4.1) per training base and test rendering; best per test rendering: Mixed test AP@50 0.636 / AP@75 0.431 ('Complete with IA' = all renderings + CDLI photographs the authors mapped onto the renderings, illumination augmentation); VL test AP@50 0.626 / AP@75 0.545 (Complete with IA) and AP@90 0.364 (VL with IA); MSII test AP@50 0.632 (Complete) / AP@75 0.437 (MSII). The release is not that 2023 working set: the Mixed zip's 1,088 sign-annotation files hold 35,130 annotations, about 28,800 of them signs on 1,070 segments of 596 tablets. Section 3 of the paper dates its tablets to 2500-2000 BC (one Old Babylonian and one Akkadian exception), and the release's translitmetadata.csv gives each tablet's CDLI period: its ED IIIb, Old Akkadian and Ur III tablets hold about 22,200 signs on 921 segments of 508 tablets (about 5% above Table 1, at the same 24 signs per segment), while about 6,600 signs on 149 segments are Old Assyrian, Old Babylonian and other tablets the paper did not use. The annotations carry no dates (creation ran until 31 August 2023, JOAD 2025) and the paper's segment list and split are not published, so no split of the release is the paper's, and a RepPoints baseline retrained on the release need not land near Table 2 (one reproduction on all segments, #254, unverified: Mixed-to-Mixed AP@50 0.80 with the paper's AP definition, against 0.609). A June 2026 eBL paper (arXiv 2606.22608, DETR on a separate photo dataset of Lewenstein et al. 2026) does not evaluate on MaiCuBeDa and releases only weights and inference outputs on Zenodo, so MaiCuBeDa remains the only open, checkable benchmark. Status 2026-10-08 (vetter): no published result found exceeding the 2023 Table 2 values on MaiCuBeDa (the JOAD 2025 dataset paper cites only the 2023 baselines); heiDATA's HTML landing page is behind the Anubis bot filter, but the Dataverse API and /api/access/datafile/{id} return 200 to plain curl GET (HEAD returns 503; ranged GET returns 206), verified on readme.txt, utils.zip and the first KB of both large zips.

## What counts as a win

On MaiCuBeDa version 1.1 (heiDATA file ids and MD5s recorded), the claimant names one test rendering R in {Mixed, VirtualLight, MSII} and uses this entry's FIXED SPLIT (set 2026-10-29, the same for every claim). Its units are the segments whose whole-segment image the sign annotation files charannotations/*.json target, one heidIcon IIIF image per segment; the back files of HS_1139, HS_1333 and HS_1340 target their front images and are left out. It keeps only Hilprecht tablets (files HS_*) whose CDLI period in the release's translitmetadata.csv (Time_period) is ED IIIb, Old Akkadian or Ur III, the paper's 2500-2000 BC range, and counts as signs the annotations classified Character or carrying a transliteration, not classified Wedge, that target such an image: 921 segments of 508 tablets with about 22,200 signs. A segment goes to test if the first hexadecimal digit of the sha256 of its IIIF identifier (the path segment after /iiif/2/ as ASCII, e.g. 1126689:591075) is 0-3, to val if it is 4-7 and to train otherwise (469 train, 241 val and 211 test segments in version 1.1); for VirtualLight and MSII the segment's HeiCuBeDa side view takes the place of its IIIF image. The claimant publishes a deterministic 512x512 patch-cropping rule and freezes (hash) the patch list and the sha256 of every whole-segment image used, before any training; no patch and no image may occur in two splits. Because the release is not the paper's working set, Table 2 is context, not the bar: the bar is a reproduced baseline. The claimant retrains the paper's RepPoints/ResNet-18 baseline (section 2.2: ResNet-18 c4 without FPN, k = 9, IoU thresholds 0.6 / 0.7, loss weights 50 / 100 / 1, SGD learning rate 5e-4, momentum 0.9, weight decay 1e-5, the stated dropout, NMS at 0.4 IoU, best epoch on val) on the fixed train split, with the training base of one pure-rendering Table 2 row (VL, MSII or Mixed renderings), and freezes its config, training log and detections (hash). A detector trained ONLY on the train split (val for model selection) produces a frozen class-agnostic detections file (COCO JSON, hash committed before scoring). Both files are scored on the test patches of rendering R with VOC 11-point interpolated AP (Everingham et al. 2010, the definition of Table 2, section 4.1 of arXiv 2308.11277), pycocotools AP reported alongside. The bar for each of AP@50 and AP@75 is the higher of the claim's own reproduced baseline and the best baseline already accepted on this split for rendering R, whatever its training base (the reproduced baselines in merged packages under known-knowns/cuneiform-sign-detection-maicubeda/; none on 2026-10-29). A full win needs AP@50 AND AP@75 each at least 0.02 above the bar, and the claim's baseline AP@50 at least that Table 2 row's value minus 0.03 (a baseline weaker than the paper's is not a faithful reproduction). Evaluation is sign-class-agnostic (localisation only) at 512x512 patch level, with the fixed split's sign filter applied to both files.

## Partial wins

- **p1:** Same protocol (fixed split, frozen detections, reproduced baseline with the same floor, the same bar), beating the bar by at least 0.04 AP@50 with AP@75 not below the bar's, or beating by at least 0.05 on VirtualLight test patches the VirtualLight bar's AP@90 (the higher of the claim's reproduced VirtualLight baseline and the best one already accepted on this split).
- **p2:** Cross-rendering generalisation: a detector trained only on one rendering type (e.g. MSII) scores AP@50 on the held-out test patches of another rendering (e.g. VirtualLight) at least 0.05 above the bar for that train-to-test direction (the higher of the RepPoints baseline the claim reproduces in that direction and the best one already accepted on this split), with frozen detections, the fixed split, and the same baseline floor against that direction's Table 2 row.

The verifier downloads via plain curl GET the sign annotation files (charannotations/*.json from any MaiCuBeDa rendering zip, about 5 MB contiguous in the Mixed zip, ranged GET on /api/access/datafile/{id}; HEAD does not work) and the whole-segment images of rendering R that the claim's hash list names, since the rendering zips hold only crops: for Mixed the heidIcon IIIF images that the annotation targets name (about 920 GETs for the fixed split, about 150 MB; robots.txt allows /iiif/), for VirtualLight and MSII the matching side views in HeiCuBeDa Hilprecht (doi:10.11588/data/IE8CCN; HeiCuBeDa_A2_Images_Sideviews_VirtualLight.zip and HeiCuBeDa_A1_Images_Sideviews_MSII_Filter.zip, about 1 to 2 MB per image, read member by member from their central directories with ranged GETs). It verifies the hashes, regenerates the fixed split from this entry's rule (469 / 241 / 211 segments) and the patch list from the published cropping rule, checks that both frozen detections files' hashes predate scoring, checks the baseline's config and training log against section 2.2 of the paper, takes the bar from the claim's baseline and any baseline already accepted on this split, rescores both files (new system and reproduced baseline) with its own VOC 11-point AP code and with pycocotools, and checks that no patch or image occurs in two splits. No retraining by the verifier; under one hour on a 10-core Mac with a normal connection (VirtualLight and MSII images add about 0.5 GB for the test split).

## Ideas to start from

Starting points, not instructions. The best wins came from angles nobody listed.

- Small anchor-free detectors (YOLO-n/s, FCOS-lite) at 512 px train in CPU-hours on 10k patches; sign boxes are small and dense, so tune anchor-free heads and NMS thresholds for crowded scenes.
- Multi-rendering fusion: stack MSII curvature, VirtualLight and Mixed renderings of the same patch as channels (the paper trains them separately).
- Test-time augmentation with illumination rotations (the dataset's VirtualLight directions) and box-level weighted fusion directly targets AP@75/AP@90.
- Use the wedge-level annotations (4,700 wedges in the dataset) as auxiliary supervision for sign boundaries.
- Reproduce the RepPoints baseline first on the frozen split; without it no comparison survives the CRITERION skeptic.

## Traps

False wins others have fallen for. A claim that walks into one of these will not survive the skeptics.

- The paper's exact split and segment list are not shipped, and the release is a larger set than the paper's working set (873 segments, 21,228 signs), mostly by tablets outside the paper's 2500-2000 BC range (the fixed split's period filter on translitmetadata.csv Time_period comes within about 5%), so Table 2 numbers are not like-for-like on any split of the release; only the reproduced baseline makes a comparison. 'Complete' and 'Photos' rows use CDLI photographs that the authors mapped onto the renderings with the Cuneur Transformer, and 'VL with IA' uses 7,344 extra VirtualLight renderings; neither is released, so only the pure-rendering rows can be reproduced from released files.
- Patches overlap at segment level: a random patch-level split leaks near-identical crops into test and inflates AP by 10+ points. Split by segment (as the paper does) and verify no patch overlap across splits.
- AP numbers depend on the AP definition, the IoU protocol and which annotations are counted. Table 2 is VOC 11-point interpolated AP (section 4.1: 'we use the interpolated precision/recall curve with 11 recall levels', citing Everingham et al. 2010), not the pycocotools 101-point default named in the criterion; 18 of its 28 AP@90 values and 4 of its 28 AP@75 values are exact multiples of 1/11 (0.364 = 4/11, 0.545 = 6/11). Score the same frozen detections both ways, say which number is set against Table 2, and document any annotation filtering.
- The rendering zips contain no whole-segment rendering, so the 512x512 patches cannot be cut from the rendering zips. Their central directories (listed 2026-10-18) hold only PNG crops (85,821 to 86,711 per zip in char/, croppoly_char/, word/, line/, wedge/ and croppoly_wedge/, each named after its parent segment) and 3,258 to 3,282 JSON annotation files (charannotations/, lineannotations/, wordannotations/). The 1,080 sign annotation files (charannotations/*.json) present in all three zips are byte-identical, and their targets name one heidIcon IIIF image per segment (heidicon.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/iiif/2/...), the Mixed rendering (the dataset readme: 'The mixed version is also available using the IIIF service heidIcon'). Whole-segment VirtualLight and MSII images are the HeiCuBeDa Hilprecht side views (doi:10.11588/data/IE8CCN, A2 and A1 zips, e.g. HS_1153_HeiCuBeDa_GMOCF_SolidColor_03_front.png): for HS_1153_03_front all three sources are 1088x1990 and reproduce the dataset's own crops (correlation 0.9996 to 0.9999, checked 2026-10-28). Name and hash the images of rendering R.
- Not every annotation is a sign: charannotations/HS_0044_03_front.png.json (the same file in all three zips) holds four editor test annotations ('Ein Polygon!', 'Ein Rechteck!', 'Knie', 'Fuss') with no image source, so 'all annotations counted' also counts non-signs. The fixed split's sign filter removes them; apply it identically to both detections files. The Mixed zip's charannotations/ also holds 8 files that are not Hilprecht tablets (six Haft Tappeh 'HT_' files and the front and back of one Kurth tablet; 319 annotations, 282 of them on gitlab.rlp.net images and 37 on one heidIcon photograph), 21 annotations with no target image, and front and back files of HS_1139, HS_1333 and HS_1340 that target the same image; the fixed split leaves those three back files out, so no image lands in two splits.
- Training-time augmentation that uses the test rendering (e.g. VirtualLight directions reserved for test) is leakage.
- The eBL 2026 DETR dataset is not released; its numbers cannot be used as a record or checked here.
- heiDATA HEAD requests return 503 and the HTML landing page sits behind a bot filter: use plain GET (ranged if needed) on /api/access/datafile/{id}; never retry through a challenge page.
