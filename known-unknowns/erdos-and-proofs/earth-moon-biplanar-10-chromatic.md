# MOONSHOT - Earth-Moon problem: a biplanar (thickness-2) graph with chromatic number 10

✏️ **Erdős problems and proofs** · moonshot (famous, long odds) · tractability ★☆☆☆☆ · compute: laptop-hours · open as of 2026-10-28

## The problem

Ringel's Earth-Moon problem asks for the largest chromatic number of a biplanar graph (edges split into two planar graphs). Euler's formula gives an upper bound of 12; Sulanke (1980, via Gardner) found a 9-chromatic biplanar graph (the join of K_6 and C_5, 11 vertices); Gethner conjectures the answer is 11. Tao's constant 27b lists 9 <= C_27b <= 12, and Epoch AI's FrontierMath open-problems page lists 'construct a biplanar graph with chromatic number at least 10' as unsolved. Target: an explicit biplanar graph with chromatic number at least 10.

## What counts as a win

Success means an explicit graph G with an explicit partition of its edges into E1 and E2, such that (i) (V,E1) and (V,E2) are each certified planar by two independent planarity testers (e.g. networkx check_planarity and Boost/OGDF, with the combinatorial embeddings saved and re-checked by Euler's formula on the rotation systems), and (ii) G is not 9-colourable, proven by a SAT solver's UNSAT result with a DRAT/LRAT proof checked by drat-trim or cake_lpr on a CNF regenerated by the skeptic. The live pages https://teorth.github.io/optimizationproblems/constants/27b.html and https://epoch.ai/frontiermath/open-problems/earth-moon must still list 9 as the best lower bound / the problem as unsolved, re-fetched live immediately before claiming.

## Partial wins

- **p1:** A biplanar graph with fractional chromatic number strictly greater than 8.5 (the value for K_6 + C_5) and above any value found in a literature search on fractional colourings of thickness-two graphs, certified by an exact rational LP solution of the fractional colouring LP and its dual.
- **p2:** A 9-critical biplanar graph not isomorphic to any graph in Boutin-Gethner-Sulanke's published lists (checked by canonical labelling), with exact verification of criticality and biplanarity.

## How a win is checked

Check each edge class for planarity (two independent implementations, plus Euler-formula check on the produced embeddings); check that E1 and E2 partition E; build the 9-colouring CNF with symmetry breaking on a clique, solve with proof logging, verify the proof. Minutes to an hour for graphs up to a few hundred vertices.

## Ideas to start from

Starting points, not instructions. The best wins came from angles nobody listed.

- Search graphs with independence number small relative to n: chi >= n / alpha, so a biplanar graph with n > 9 alpha forces 10 colours; biplanar graphs have fewer than 6n edges, so search dense near-6n-edge graphs (blow-ups and joins of planar triangulations) with small alpha via SAT.
- Join constructions: K_6 + C_5 is a join; search joins and generalised joins of small planar pieces whose two planar layers can absorb the cross edges, using SAT with planarity encoded through Schnyder woods or a fixed rotation system.
- Cross-domain import (VLSI two-layer routing): a biplanar graph is a two-layer wiring with no vias; reuse two-layer routing heuristics to decide whether a candidate dense graph splits into two planar layers.
- Cross-domain import (frequency assignment in cellular networks): interference graphs of two overlaid planar cell layouts are biplanar; known hard instances for frequency assignment may contain high chromatic structure.
- Fractional relaxation first (partial win p1): maximise n/alpha-type LP bounds, which guide the search toward graphs that can possibly need 10 colours.

## Traps

False wins others have fallen for. A claim that walks into one of these will not survive the skeptics.

- Thickness vs biplanarity on the sphere vs plane is the same, but 'Earth-Moon' variants with countries of several regions are different problems; the target is graphs of thickness at most 2.
- A planarity claim from a drawing is not a proof; use two independent planarity algorithms and check the embeddings.
- Unverified UNSAT: a solver saying 'not 9-colourable' must come with a checked DRAT/LRAT proof.
- Multigraph edges or loops created by merging vertices are not allowed; the graph must be simple.
