# The last open case of the r-self-orthogonal Latin square spectrum: a 193-SOLS of order 14

📊 **Record tables** · standard · tractability ★★☆☆☆ · compute: laptop-days (SAT or local search on a 14x14 square) · open as of 2026-10-30

## The problem

Superimposing a Latin square A of order n on its transpose gives the n^2 ordered pairs (A[i][j], A[j][i]). A is r-self-orthogonal (an r-SOLS(n)) if exactly r of these pairs are distinct. Xu and Chang (Discrete Math. 2006) and Hantao Zhang ('25 new r-self-orthogonal Latin squares', Discrete Math. 313 (2013) 1746-1753) proved that an r-SOLS(n) exists iff n <= r <= n^2 and r is not n+1 or n^2-1, apart from 26 genuine exceptions with n <= 7 and one possible exception, (n, r) = (14, 14^2 - 3) = (14, 193). Sergey Bereg (arXiv:2311.00992 v3, Feb 2024; COCOA 2023) calls finding a 193-self-orthogonal Latin square of order 14 the only case left to complete the spectrum, and reports that values of r close to n^2 are the hard ones. No later construction or non-existence proof was found in arXiv listings through Oct 2026.

## What counts as a win

EITHER (existence) an explicit 14x14 Latin square A over {0..13} such that the set {(A[i][j], A[j][i]) : 0 <= i,j < 14} has exactly 193 elements, checked by two independent programs; OR (non-existence) a proof that none exists, delivered as a DRAT/LRAT-verified UNSAT certificate of a CNF that the verifier regenerates from a published encoding, with any symmetry-breaking justified in writing. Before claiming, re-check Bereg's arXiv:2311.00992 and search arXiv and the literature for a construction of a 193-SOLS(14) published since.

## Partial wins

- **p1:** A DRAT/LRAT-verified proof that no 193-SOLS(14) exists under a stated structural restriction that is not already in the literature (for example A idempotent, or A invariant under a given automorphism such as x -> x+2 mod 14 on rows, columns and symbols), with the CNF regenerated by the verifier.

## How a win is checked

Check each row and column of A is a permutation of 0..13. Count the distinct ordered pairs (A[i][j], A[j][i]) over all 196 cells and require exactly 193. For non-existence, regenerate the CNF from the published encoder and check the certificate with drat-trim or cake_lpr.
