# Erdős unit distance problem for small n: more unit distances among n points than the best known (n = 22..100)

## The problem

u(n) is the maximum number of pairs at distance exactly 1 among n points in the plane (Erdős Problem #90, OEIS A186705). Alexeev, Mixon and Parshall (arXiv:2412.11914, v2 Feb 2025) determined u(n) exactly for n <= 21 and proved upper bounds for 22 <= n <= 30; the best lower bounds are u(22) >= 60, u(23) >= 64, u(24) >= 68, u(25) >= 72, u(26) >= 76, u(27) >= 81, u(28) >= 85, u(29) >= 89, u(30) >= 93 (from Schade, Ágoston-Pálvölgyi and Engel et al.), against upper bounds 61, 66, 72, 78, 84, 90, 96, 103, 110. Engel, Hammond-Lee, Su, Varga and Zsámboki (arXiv:2406.15317) gave lower bounds for 30 < n <= 100 by beam search in the Moser ring. The asymptotic problem moved in 2026 (erdosproblems.com/90 now marks Erdős's n^(1+o(1)) conjecture disproved, via an OpenAI-hosted proof with details in Will Sawin's arXiv:2605.20579 and remarks by Alon et al. in arXiv:2605.20695), but small-n values are separate and still open. Find n points with more unit distances than the best known lower bound for some n in 22..100.

## What counts as a win

Success means n explicit points in the plane, given exactly (coordinates in a stated number field, e.g. Q(sqrt3, sqrt11, ...) or as an algebraic embedding certificate), with E pairs at distance exactly 1 verified by exact arithmetic in two independent implementations, where E is STRICTLY GREATER (direction: max) than the best known lower bound for u(n) at verification time: the maximum over https://oeis.org/A186705 re-fetched live immediately before claiming, arXiv:2412.11914 (latest version), arXiv:2406.15317 (latest version), https://www.erdosproblems.com/90, and a fresh arXiv search. E must not exceed the proved upper bounds in arXiv:2412.11914 for n <= 30.

## Partial wins

- **p1:** Settle u(22) (is it 60 or 61?) or u(23): either 22 points with 61 unit distances, verified exactly, or an independently re-runnable exhaustive computation extending the Alexeev-Mixon-Parshall method showing 61 is impossible.
- **p2:** A unit-distance graph for some 31 <= n <= 100 that matches or beats the Engel et al. lower bound for that n, verified exactly, whose embedding is not an isometric copy of a Moser-ring configuration: certified by exhibiting two unit edge vectors u, v (as complex numbers) such that the ratio v/u lies neither in the Moser ring (as defined in Engel et al., arXiv:2406.15317) nor in its complex conjugate, with the ring-membership test done in exact arithmetic and re-runnable.

## How a win is checked

Represent coordinates exactly (sympy algebraic numbers or a number-field library). For every pair, compute the squared distance exactly and count pairs equal to 1; check all points are distinct. Two independent implementations (e.g. sympy and PARI/GP or Sage) must agree. Floating-point checks (|d-1| < 1e-12) may be used only as a filter, never as proof. Compare with the live OEIS page and the latest versions of the two arXiv papers.

## Ideas to start from

Starting points, not instructions. The best wins came from angles nobody listed.

- Search rings beyond the Moser ring: point sets in rings generated by several unit-length rotations (unit complex numbers in fields such as Q(sqrt(-3), sqrt(-11)) or Q(sqrt(-3), sqrt(-15))). The 17-vertex exception found by Alexeev-Mixon-Parshall shows good graphs exist outside the Moser ring.
- From number theory (the 2026 asymptotic breakthroughs): points in lattices of number fields with many elements of norm 1. Enumerate small 'unit-distance-rich' subsets of such lattices for n up to 100.
- Combinatorial-first: generate dense graphs that avoid known non-embeddable subgraphs (K4, K_{2,3}, the forbidden family F of Alexeev-Mixon-Parshall), then test embeddability with exact algebra, as in their pipeline.
- Minkowski sums and unions of rotated copies of good small configurations (Hamming graph H(3,3) gives 27 points with 81 unit distances) followed by greedy point addition/removal.
- Beam search (Engel et al.) with different scoring, e.g. reward for future potential edges, and with different generating rotation sets.

## Traps

False wins others have fallen for. A claim that walks into one of these will not survive the skeptics.

- Floating-point 'unit distances' that are only approximately 1. Use exact arithmetic; many near-coincidences exist in dense configurations.
- Duplicate points: coincident points can inflate the count. Check distinctness exactly.
- Exceeding a proved upper bound for n <= 30 means the checker is wrong.
- The asymptotic result (n^(1+c) unit distances for infinitely many n) does not give small-n records; compare per n. erdosproblems.com/90 says 'disproved' for the asymptotic conjecture only.
- Comparing with OEIS A186705 data alone: OEIS lists only exact values to n = 21; the lower bounds for n >= 22 live in the papers.
